# Phase 2 (After Day 28) — Ongoing Hypertrophy Program

## Why Phase 2 exists

The 28-day plan (Weeks 1–4) is an **introductory block**: its job is to get
your joints, tendons, and nervous system used to loaded training again
without getting hurt. It deliberately uses low weekly volume (about 3
working sets per muscle group per week) and caps itself with a taper —
that's the right call for week 1, but it is **not enough stimulus for
ongoing muscle growth**. Left on a permanent loop, it plateaus fast.

Phase 2 is what you move into once Day 28 is done. It's a **6-day
Push/Pull/Legs split, repeated twice a week**, so each muscle group is
trained twice weekly instead of once — the single biggest lever for
hypertrophy at a given weekly volume. It's still 100% home-based: dumbbells/
kettlebells, the stationary bike, and a step. No new equipment required.

## Structure

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| 1 | Push A |
| 2 | Pull A |
| 3 | Legs A |
| 4 | Push B |
| 5 | Pull B |
| 6 | Legs B |
| 7 | Rest |

The A/B variants aren't just repeats — they hit each muscle group from a
different angle/exercise selection across the week, which is standard
practice for balanced hypertrophy. See [phase-2-week.md](phase-2-week.md)
for the full exercise list.

**Only 5 days available some weeks?** Merge Legs A and Legs B into a
single, slightly longer Legs day (do all 6 leg exercises at 2 sets each
instead of 3x each split across two days), and run Push A/Pull A/Push B/
Pull B/Legs(combined) across 5 days with 2 rest days.

## How progression works: double progression

Each exercise lists a rep range (e.g. "8–12"), not a fixed number. This is
how you keep progressing for months without needing ever-heavier dumbbells
overnight:

1. Pick a weight you can do for at least the **bottom** of the range
   (e.g. 8 reps) on all 3 sets with clean form.
2. Each session, try to add a rep per set, working up toward the **top**
   of the range (e.g. 12).
3. Once you hit the top of the range for all 3 sets, increase the weight
   by the smallest increment your dumbbells allow next session, and drop
   back to the bottom of the range.
4. Log every session — see [training-log-template.md](training-log-template.md).
   Without a log, "progressive overload" quietly turns into "same weights
   forever."

**Effort level:** the last set of each exercise should be genuinely hard —
1–2 reps left in the tank, not a grinding failure rep. Sets 1–2 of 3 can be
a bit easier. This is enough stimulus without wrecking recovery.

**When your dumbbells run out of weight:** don't stall — make the same
load harder instead:
- Slow the eccentric (3–4 seconds lowering)
- Add a 1–2 second pause at the hardest point of the rep
- Switch to single-arm/single-leg versions
- Cut rest between sets by 15–20 seconds
- Push reps up to 20–25 as a "burn" set on the last set only

## Recovery rules (this matters more at 45–55, not less)

- **Deload every 5–6 weeks:** one week at ~2 sets per exercise instead of
  3, and hold your weights flat instead of pushing them up. This isn't
  optional — it's what lets tendons catch up to the muscle, which recovers
  faster than connective tissue does.
- **Rest day stays a real rest day.** No lifting, no hard bike intervals.
  An easy walk or gentle stretch is fine.
- If a joint (not a muscle) is aching going into a session, drop that
  movement for the day and sub something pain-free — don't push through
  joint pain the way you might push through muscle fatigue.
- If you're still sore from the last session for a muscle group, it's fine
  to push that day back rather than train through it.

## Where cardio fits

Keep the stationary bike for heart health, but don't let it eat into
lifting recovery:
- 1–2x/week, add a 15–20 minute **easy-to-moderate** bike spin after a
  Push or Pull session (not after Legs, and not on the rest day).
- Keep it easy enough that it doesn't leave your legs fatigued for the
  next day's lifting.
- If you want a harder cardio session, that's what the original 28-day
  plan's Day 5/12/19 style bike intervals are for — just don't stack a
  hard bike session the day before or after Legs.

## What actually drives growth beyond the program itself

Programming is maybe a third of the equation. Also needed:
- **Protein:** roughly 1.6–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day, spread across
  meals, is the range generally supported for muscle growth in adults.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. This is when the actual repair/growth happens —
  a great program on 5 hours of sleep underperforms a mediocre program on
  8.
- **Calories:** you generally can't build meaningful new muscle in a large
  calorie deficit. If fat loss is also a goal, expect slower muscle gain,
  or alternate phases (a deliberate bulk/cut) rather than chasing both
  maximally at once.

None of this is medical/dietary advice for your specific situation — check
with a doctor or dietitian if you have any conditions that affect protein
or calorie intake.
