# Overview & Rules

## Equipment assumed

- Adjustable dumbbells or kettlebells (light + heavy option if you have it)
- A stationary/static bike
- A sturdy step, stair, or low chair for step-ups
- No pull-up bar and no outdoor running required — everything below is
  built to be done entirely at home with the above.

## Weekly template

| Day | Focus | Notes |
|-----|-------|-------|
| 1 | Push | Chest/shoulders/triceps + core |
| 2 | Pull | Back/biceps + core, loaded with dumbbells instead of a bar |
| 3 | Rest / mobility | Full rest — light stretching okay |
| 4 | Legs | Low-impact (step-ups instead of jump squats early on) |
| 5 | Conditioning | Stationary-bike intervals, moderate intensity |
| 6 | Optional easy day | Easy bike spin, or full rest if sore |
| 7 | Rest | Full rest, foam rolling if available |

This repeats 4 times = 28 days. Day numbers in week-1.md through week-4.md
follow this same 1–7 pattern each week.

## Substitutions made vs. the original plan, and why

| Original move | Why it's a concern at 45–55 | Substitute used here |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-ups on a bar | Needs equipment most people don't have at home; also high shoulder-joint demand cold | Dumbbell bent-over rows / single-arm rows (loaded, no bar required) |
| Outdoor timed runs (1, 1.5, 2 miles) | Weather-dependent, uncontrolled surface/pace, hard on knees/hips without a proper base | Stationary-bike intervals and time trials — same cardio stimulus, zero impact |
| Jump squats | High knee/patellar-tendon impact, especially escalating weekly | Step-ups (Weeks 1–2), loaded goblet squats and slow tempo squat pulses (Weeks 3–4) |
| Burpees (full, with jump) | Combines high-impact landing with lower-back-loaded transition, done at volume in EMOMs | Step-back burpees (no jump) throughout; full burpees only as an optional Week 4 variant |
| Sit-ups (weighted volume) | Repeated spinal flexion under fatigue is a common driver of lower-back irritation | Dead bugs / slow bicycle crunches (control > speed) |
| Zero rest days across 28 days | No recovery window for tendons/joints to adapt; classic overuse-injury setup | 2 rest days + 1 optional easy day per week |
| 2-minute max push-up/sit-up/pull-up test (Day 25) | All-out max-rep testing under fatigue is exactly when form breaks down and injuries happen | Replaced with a controlled, form-first assessment (see week-4.md) |
| Weekly "+X reps per round" stacked on top of a no-rest schedule | Volume compounds faster than recovery can keep up | Smaller weekly increases, applied to a schedule that actually has recovery days |

## Progression rules

- Increase reps/rounds/load only when you can complete the current week's
  sessions with good form and without lingering joint soreness the next day.
- If a week feels too hard, repeat it rather than advancing — there's no
  penalty for taking 5 weeks instead of 4.
- Dumbbell/kettlebell load: pick a weight where the last 2 reps of a set
  are hard but clean. Add load in small increments (the smallest jump your
  dumbbells allow) only once a full week feels easy, rather than adding
  reps and weight in the same week.
- Plank and core work: stop the moment your lower back sags or you start
  holding your breath — a shorter clean plank beats a longer sagging one.
- It's fine (encouraged) to take an extra rest day beyond the plan if
  you're unusually sore — resume the sequence where you left off.
